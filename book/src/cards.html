How to use the cards

One a month

  1. On the first day of the month, read the chapter. Then take out the month’s card.
  2. Put it where you will see it without looking for it: the desk, the mirror, the inside of the wallet.
  3. The front is the axiom. The back is the practice, in brief. The book has the rest.
  4. Tick the boxes as you go. On the last day, return the card to the box and sign the ledger.
  5. Keep the cards after the year. The three you kept are the instruments; carry those.

Instrument one · Direction
I

The Eulogy

Begin at the end.

Month oneI of XII

Practice · Month one

Write the eulogy

  1. Choose the speaker: the person whose good opinion you most want to have deserved.
  2. Set the scene in one line. The year, the room, who is there.
  3. Four hundred words in their voice. Not what you achieved: what it was like to know you.
  4. Read it aloud once. Mark every sentence not yet true.
  5. Extract three governing lines. They now outrank every goal you have.

Instrument two · Time
II

The Weeks

A life is four thousand weeks. Spend them on purpose.

Month twoII of XII

Practice · Month two

Fill the chart

  1. Colour every week you have lived, in pen.
  2. Find the line at eighty. Count the rows left; write the number.
  3. The tail-end sum for three people: times a year, times years left.
  4. Set the weekly non-negotiable: two hours, same time, twelve weeks in the calendar.
  5. Cancel one weekly recurrence today.

Instrument three · Thinking
III

The Question

The quality of a life follows the quality of its questions.

Month threeIII of XII

Practice · Month three

Fifty questions

  1. Fifty questions about your own life, one sitting, no answering.
  2. The next day, circle the three that made you uncomfortable.
  3. For each, write the answer you are pretending not to know.
  4. Find the eigenquestion: the one whose answer settles the others.
  5. Carry it for the month. Do not force an answer.

Instrument four · Judgment
IV

Inversion

Avoid stupidity before you attempt brilliance.

Month fourIV of XII

Practice · Month four

Run the premortem

  1. Name the most important thing you are doing this year.
  2. It is one year from today. It has failed completely.
  3. Fifteen reasons, specific, in the past tense.
  4. Circle three; one action this month against each.
  5. Write the kill criterion and the not-to-do list.

Instrument five · Risk
V

Asymmetry

Make many small bets whose downside is bounded and whose upside is not.

Month fiveV of XII

Practice · Month five

Sort the bets

  1. List every decision you have been putting off.
  2. Mark each door. Decide every two-way door today.
  3. One-way doors: what would have to be true, and the cheapest way to find out.
  4. Ten small bets; place three this month.
  5. Open the decision ledger: what I expect, and when to check.

Instrument six · Learning
VI

The Loop

You become whatever you get fast feedback on.

Month sixVI of XII

Practice · Month six

Shorten the loop

  1. Name the three skills your governing lines require.
  2. Measure each loop: how long until you know honestly how it went.
  3. Take the longest and design it ten times shorter.
  4. Run it for thirty days. Count the iterations.
  5. Retrieve, do not re-read: write what the chapter said before opening it.

Instrument seven · Focus
VII

Attention

Your experience is what you agree to attend to.

Month sevenVII of XII

Practice · Month seven

The thousand minutes

  1. Three days in ten-minute boxes, one word each.
  2. Colour what you would choose again. Count the rest.
  3. Sacred hours: two a day, same time, phone in another room.
  4. Close every session in one line: where I stopped, the next step.
  5. Remove one input for the month.

Instrument eight · Patience
VIII

Compounding

Small, repeated, for years, beats large, occasional, for months.

Month eightVIII of XII

Practice · Month eight

The ten-year letter

  1. Name the one asset that, held ten years, changes everything.
  2. Write the letter, dated ten years from today, from you to you.
  3. Set the weekly minimum you could sustain for five hundred and twenty weeks.
  4. Name what compounds against you. Start reducing it.
  5. Keep the letter in the book. The envelope will ask.

Practice · Month nine

One hundred ideas

  2. The next day, circle five by asymmetry.

  4. Start the commonplace book: one page a day.

Instrument ten · Courage
X

The Ask

Most of the world is a door that opens if you knock.

Month tenX of XII

Practice · Month ten

Five asks in seven days

  1. List ten asks you have been avoiding.
  2. Rank by asymmetry. Pick five.
  3. Ask within seven days. Write the answer, not the fear.
  4. Write the letter you have been not writing. Send it.
  5. Collect ten refusals before the month ends.

Instrument eleven · Energy
XI

The Body

The mind is something the body does.

Month elevenXI of XII

Practice · Month eleven

Fourteen days of the four

  1. Fixed wake time. The alarm is for bed, seven and a half hours before.
  2. Daylight within an hour of waking. Ten minutes outdoors.
  3. Thirty minutes of movement a day; three hard sessions a fortnight.
  4. Five minutes of slow breathing when arousal is high.
  5. Mark the grid nightly. Day fifteen: what changed?

Instrument twelve · Relationships
XII

The Others

Good relationships keep us happier and healthier. Period.

Month twelveXII of XII

Practice · Month twelve

The gratitude visit

  1. Name the five. Beside each, the date of the last real conversation.
  2. Three hundred words of gratitude to one of them.
  3. Deliver it in person, and read it aloud.
  4. The weekly call: one of the five, thirty minutes, twelve weeks.
  5. Repair one thing. Have the postponed conversation.
