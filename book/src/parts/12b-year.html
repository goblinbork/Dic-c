After the year

The year was a test of twelve instruments against one life, and its result is not in this book. It is in the twelve ledger pages in your handwriting, and specifically in the last line of each, where you wrote kept or revised or nothing. Nobody keeps twelve. Most people keep three, and the three are different for each person, and there was no way to know which they would be without running all twelve. That was the point of the order and the calendar: you had to be made to try the ones you would have skipped.

Look at the ledgers and name your three. They are the instruments you now use without deciding to, that would feel strange to stop. Keep those and let the other nine go with thanks; any of them can be rented again in a month that needs it. Keep three other things regardless. Keep the column, whatever it has become, because it is the vehicle everything else rides in. Keep the decision ledger, and read the entries you made nine months ago, because that is the only loop that tells you what you are actually good at. And keep the vocabulary, which is now yours: governing lines, the column, the fifty, the kill criterion, the asymmetry sort, loop length, the thousand minutes, the flat part, the gap, the bridge, the four, the five. They are short words for long arguments, and they let you think the arguments in the time it takes to say them.

Then there is what the book cannot do, which is most things. It cannot make you rich, promoted, loved or well, and the books that promise those are the ones that do not work. What a year of use changes is smaller and more durable: what you notice, what you count, what you ask, whom you call, and what you do on Thursdays. Everything larger is built out of those, by you, over years, on the flat part of a curve you can now at least recognise.

The second year fits in five lines. Read the eulogy again on the anniversary and rewrite it; the difference between the drafts is the only measure of the year that matters. Choose the three. Set the column for another fifty-two weeks. Re-read the vow on the first day of each quarter and mark it. And when you meet the person this copy should belong to next, write their name in the colophon and give it to them with everything in the box.

One instrument remains. Turn the page and sign it.
