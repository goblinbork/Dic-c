The Vocabulary

The book’s tools, in the order they appear. Borrowed terms are credited; the rest are this book’s, and are now yours.

Governing lines I
Three past-tense sentences from your eulogy you cannot yet claim. They outrank goals.
The column II
A weekly non-negotiable, seen as a column running down the years of the chart.
The tail-end sum II
Times a year you see someone, times the years left. After Tim Urban.
The fifty III
Fifty questions about your own life in one sitting. The last ten are yours.
The eigenquestion III
The question whose answer settles the others. The term is Shishir Mehrotra’s.
Kill criterion IV
A signal and a date, fixed in advance, at which you stop. After Annie Duke.
The four failures IV
Energy, attention, bet size, people. Instruments XI, VII, V and XII answer them.
The not-to-do list IV
Five refusals that keep a year from being wasted. Works on bad days.
The asymmetry sort V
Bets (take many), chores (decide fast), wagers (size small), ruin (never).
Three bankrolls V
Money, time, reputation. Never stake what makes the next bet impossible.
The decision ledger V
What you expect, written when you decide; compared nine months on. After Drucker.
Loop length VI
Time from an action to an honest signal about it. Predicts learning better than talent.
The loop ratio VI
Honest iterations a year. Cut loop length by ten; it multiplies by ten.
The applause trap VI
Feedback that is fast and flattering. Worse than none.
The thousand minutes VII
A waking day in a hundred boxes of ten. Counted minutes get chosen.
The room rule VII
The phone goes in another room. Distance does what willpower cannot.
The closure line VII
One line at the end of a session: where I stopped, what comes next.
The flat part VIII
The years in which compounding looks like nothing. Where most people leave.
The compounding audit VIII
Quarterly: each recurring hour marked compounding, neutral or decaying.
The weekly minimum VIII
The smallest unit of the asset you can sustain for five hundred and twenty weeks.
The gap IX
The distance between taste and ability, closed only by volume. After Ira Glass.
The make/consume ratio IX
Hours made against hours consumed. One to one for a month.
The second version IX
Do not polish the first draft. Make the second.
The bridge X
A weak tie across a gap between groups. Better ideas live there. After Burt.
Collecting refusals X
Aim for a number of noes; the yeses arrive on the way.
The four XI
Wake time, morning light, thirty minutes moving, five minutes breathing. Decided once.
The five XII
The people you would call at three in the morning, with the date of the last real talk.
The small turn XII
Turning toward a bid for attention rather than away. After John Gottman.
