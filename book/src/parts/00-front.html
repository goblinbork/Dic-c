Before you begin

Most books do not work. This is an odd thing to print at the front of one, but it is true, and it is the reason this book is shaped the way it is. A book rests on an unspoken theory of learning: that an author can think a thought, write it down, and a reader can absorb it by reading. The theory is wrong. Almost everyone who finishes a serious book retains very little of it a month later, and almost nobody notices. The words go in, feel like understanding, and go out again. You have probably read a hundred books that were supposed to change your life. Count the ones that did.

The ones that did have something in common. You did something with them. You copied a sentence out, you tried a method, you argued with a chapter for a week, you gave a copy to someone and had to explain it. The change never came from the reading. It came from the use.

So this is not a book to be read. It is an instrument to be used, and it has been built accordingly. It is short, so that it can be read again. It is divided into twelve, so that it can be practised, one instrument a month, for a year. It is printed on paper that takes ink, with pages that are meant to be written on. It is numbered, so that it will be kept. And it is expensive, so that it will not be ignored. That last point is not a joke. Things we pay dearly for, we use; the price is part of the mechanism.

The word instrument carries three meanings, and the book means all of them. An instrument is a tool, made for a purpose, that extends what a hand can do. An instrument is something you practise, badly at first, until it becomes an extension of you. And an instrument, in the oldest sense, is a document with binding force: a deed, a contract, a will. There is a page near the end of this book that asks for your signature. It is there because a book you have signed is harder to put down than one you have merely bought.

The twelve

None of the twelve instruments is a secret, and none of them is new. Some are two thousand years old. Each has been chosen because it satisfies three tests: it changes what a person does, not only what they think; the change lasts; and there is evidence, from research or from long human practice, that it works for people who are not unusual. Where an instrument leans on research, the research is named in the notes at the back, together with what it does not prove. Where it leans on a story, the story is labelled a story. The claim of this book is not that its ideas are original. It is that a year of using them is.

How to use it

  1. One instrument a month, in order. Read the chapter on the first day of the month. It takes twenty minutes. Do not read ahead. The instruments build on one another, and anticipation is part of the method.
  2. Do the practice within the first seven days. By hand, with a pen, on the ledger page that faces it. Handwriting is slow, and slowness is the point: you cannot write a sentence by hand without deciding whether you mean it.
  3. Carry the card for that instrument for the rest of the month. Put it where you will see it without looking for it: the desk, the mirror, the inside of the wallet. It is there to interrupt you.
  4. On the last day of the month, return to the ledger page. Sign it, date it, and mark it kept or revised. Both are honourable. Blank is not.
  5. After the twelfth month, sign the vow, and then open the sealed envelope that came with the book. Not before.
  6. If you miss a month, you have not failed. Begin again on the next first day. The book does not mind. The book is patient in a way that you are not yet, and that is one of the things it is for.
  7. Do not lend the book during the year. After the year, do. The colophon has room for the names of the people it belongs to next.

You will notice the book does not ask what you want. It asks what you will regret, what you attend to, what you would have to be true, whom you would knock for. These are stranger questions and they have better answers. Begin with the first one.

The set

This copy came in a box with five other things. None of them is decoration. Each has a job in the method, and each is named where it is needed.

  1. The book

    Ninety-six pages. Twelve instruments, each with a practice and a ledger page meant to be written on, in pen. The paper was chosen for it.

  2. The twelve cards

    One card per instrument, printed letterpress, with the axiom on one face and the practice on the other. Carry the month’s card where you will see it without looking for it.

  3. The weeks chart

    Four thousand six hundred and eighty boxes, one per week of a ninety-year life, with the eightieth year marked, folded like a map. It is filled in during the second month and consulted at every review after.

  4. The needle

    A brass page-marker stamped with this copy’s number. It marks the current month. When the year is finished, it marks the vow.

  5. The sealed envelope

    The thirteenth chapter. It is opened after the vow is signed, and not before. Owners who have opened it early report that it did not work, which is the truth.

  6. The register

    Each copy can be registered with the publisher by its number. The register records the day your year began and sends one message on the first of each month, with nothing in it but the instrument’s name. When the book is passed on, the next owner registers it again, and the year begins again.
